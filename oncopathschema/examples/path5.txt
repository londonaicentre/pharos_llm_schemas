HISTOLOGY
  As reported by my Haematopathology specialist colleagues  and at , the specimen consists of two cores of densely fibrous breast
  tissue showing infiltration by predominantly small lymphocytes with a vaguely
  nodular growth pattern. Some lymphocytes show plasmacytoid differentiation and
  there are admixed mature plasma cells plus a few eosinophils. Larger blasts
  are s
een admixed, but also focally forming aggregates. There are residual
  ductal structures, but no lympho-epithelial lesions are seen.
  The combined immunohistochemistry results obtained at and ,
  indicate that thelymphocytes are positive for CD20, CD79a and BCL2 but are
  negative for CD5, CD10 and cyclin D1. There is patchy expression of CD23 and a
  proportion of cells, mainly among the larger ones, express IRF4/MUM1, BCL6 and
  CD30. A few of the largerblasts are also CD10 positive and have an underlying
  FDC meshwork; these are therefore interpreted as overrun follicles rather than
  areas of transformation although the MUM1 expression is unusual. CD138 is also
  expressed in a few areas of greatest confluence of larger cells and these
  cells can be seen slightly more easily in the H&E stains performed at St
  Thomas' to be part of the plasmacytoid/plasma cell component of the tumour.
  Unfortunately, attempted kappa and lambda immunohistochemistry wa
s
  uninformative. The proliferative fraction varies but is generally low,
  approximately 20%.
  The features are those of extranodal marginal zone lymphoma and there is
  nothing at this site to indicate high grade progression/transformation.
DIAGNOSIS
  Left breast (core biopsy): Extranodal marginal zone B cell lymphoma, presumed
                              to be of MALT type.
